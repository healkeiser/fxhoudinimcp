## Particle Separation is the single quality dial

**Particle Separation on mpmcontainer drives the quality of the sources, the
colliders and the solver at once.** Smaller means more detail, exactly as with
FLIP. It is the parameter to change when quality is wrong, and the parameter to
leave coarse while establishing behaviour.

## Colliders, which is where MPM setups usually go wrong

- Use **Animated (Rigid)** Collider Type whenever the collider allows it. It interpolates precisely between frames, and it is often enough on its own: the spinning tire example moves material by friction alone, with no custom velocity field.
- **Particles passing through a thin collider** has three escalating fixes, in this order:
  1. Raise **Voxel Size** in the mpmcollider's Geometry section. This overrides the global resolution so that one collider gets a finer mesh, which is cheaper than raising quality globally.
  2. Turn on **Enable Particle-Level Collisions** on the solver's Advanced tab. A second collision step runs directly on the material points: more computation, more accurate.
  3. Choose **Velocity-Based Move Outside Colliders**. This adjusts particle velocity so particles end up outside the collider after the next integration step. It is the most accurate option and it can introduce instability, which is why it is last.

## Emission

**Stepping artifacts in a stream** are a substep problem, not a source problem.
Raise **Global Substeps** in the solver's Solver > Iterations section for smooth
continuous emission.

## Judgement

- MPM is not a drop-in replacement for FLIP, Vellum or RBD. `mpm/mpmvsothers` explains which solver actually suits a material, and reading it before committing saves rebuilding the setup.
- MPM material points can be put to sleep; on large scenes that matters for cost. See `mpm/sleep`.
- Surfacing is a separate stage with its own page, `mpm/surfacing`. Cache the sim before surfacing so look changes never re-solve.
- Debris sourcing and destruction interplay have their own pages, `mpm/sourcingdebris` and `mpm/destruction`.

## Order of work

1. Build the source and colliders, then `get_geometry_info` to confirm they are not empty.
2. mpmcontainer with a deliberately coarse Particle Separation.
3. Set collider types before touching anything else. Animated (Rigid) where possible.
4. `cook_frame_range(node_path, start, end)` over a short range, then `capture_screenshot`. Fix penetration with the three-step escalation above, stepping with Global Substeps.
5. `get_sim_memory_usage`, then cache, then surface.
